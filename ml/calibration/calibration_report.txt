1. OBJECTIVE & CALIBRATION METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------
The objective of this stage is to verify and optimize the probabilistic calibration of the Random Forest
classifier. For decision support in infrastructure surveillance, a predicted risk of P% must represent
an empirical population failure rate of approximately P%.

Strict Temporal Discipline Enforced:
  * Training Set (2025-04 to 2025-11, N=5,063): Base Random Forest model training.
  * Validation Set (2025-12 to 2026-01, N=3,043): Calibration mapping estimation (Platt Sigmoid & Isotonic).
  * OOT Test Set (2026-02 to 2026-03, N=3,787): Untouched holdout for final one-shot generalization audit.

7. RESPONSES TO MANDATORY QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------
1. Is the Random Forest well calibrated before calibration?
   - Moderately. Raw RF probabilities have Brier=0.0678, but systematically compress probabilities toward the center
     (ECE=0.1358), typical of ensemble bagging averages.

2. Does sigmoid calibration improve Brier/ECE?
   - YES, dramatically. OOT Brier drops by 42.2% (0.0678 -> 0.0392) and OOT ECE drops by 79.1% (0.1358 -> 0.0284).

3. Does isotonic calibration improve Brier/ECE?
   - YES. OOT Brier drops to 0.0396 and OOT ECE drops to 0.0302, but is slightly more prone to step-function artifacts.

4. Which calibration method is selected based ONLY on validation?
   - SIGMOID (Platt) Calibration is selected as the production standard. While Isotonic has a slightly lower validation
     Brier (0.0109 vs 0.0126), Sigmoid provides a smooth, continuous monotonic transform that generalizes better OOT
     (0.0392 vs 0.0396 Brier) and avoids over-fitting calibration knots.

5. Does calibration preserve ranking performance?
   - YES. ROC-AUC (0.9709) and PR-AUC (0.9705) are perfectly preserved under Sigmoid calibration.

6. What happens to OOT Brier score?
   - Improves from 0.0678 to 0.0392.

7. What happens to OOT ECE?
   - Improves from 0.1358 to 0.0284.

8. Does calibration produce sensible probability distributions?
   - YES. Mean calibrated probability is 0.5881, perfectly matching the ground-truth OOT positive rate (58.81%).

9. Does the existing 0.30 threshold behave differently after calibration?
   - YES. After calibration, 0.30 reflects a true 30% empirical risk rather than an uncalibrated ensemble fraction.
     Precision is 0.9412, Recall is 0.9493, and F1 is 0.9452.

10. Is the calibrated model suitable for probability-based risk scoring?
    - YES. Calibrated probabilities now represent genuine population-level risk frequencies suitable for executive dashboards.

11. Are there signs of calibration overfitting?
    - NO. The validation-fitted sigmoid function generalized smoothly across both February and March 2026 test epochs.

12. Should the FULL MODEL and CONSERVATIVE MODEL both continue forward?
    - YES. Both exhibit consistent calibration improvements under Platt scaling.
