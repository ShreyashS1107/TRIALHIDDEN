------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OBJECTIVE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The purpose of this component is to represent infrastructure projects not by arbitrary administrative tags or raw project
identifiers, but as multidimensional vectors of structural project characteristics and pre-transition historical dynamics.
By clustering similar projects together, we establish structural project archetypes that allow the derivation of empirical,
interpretable historical benchmarks.

Core Design Principles:
1. Pure Causal Isolation: All clustering variables are derived strictly from baseline project parameters and pre-PAIMANA
   historical records ($t_{\text{evidence}} \le \text{March 2025}$).
2. Strict Prohibition of Identifiers & Future Outcomes: Project IDs, project names, and future performance metrics are
   explicitly excluded from feature representation.
3. Maximum Explainability: Archetypes must possess intuitive physical meaning suitable for presentation to MoSPI / IPMD
   leadership and hackathon judges.


========================================================================================================================
2. FEATURE REPRESENTATION MATRIX
========================================================================================================================

The longitudinal clustering representation vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ combines two complementary domains:

A. Static / Baseline Structural Dimensions:
1. `original_cost_log`: $\ln(1 + \text{original\_cost\_crore})$. Normalizes heavy right-skewed infrastructure budget distributions
   (ranging from ₹150 Cr to ₹50,000+ Cr).
2. `planned_duration_months`: Sanctioned timeline from `approval_start_date` to `original_completion_date` in months.
3. `project_size_category`: Mega (>= ₹1,000 Cr) vs Major (₹150–₹1,000 Cr).
4. `primary_sector`: High-level domain (Roads/Highways, Railways, Energy/Petroleum, Power, Coal, Urban).

B. Historical Pre-Transition Behavioral Dimensions (Strictly as-of April 2025):
5. `pre_paimana_observation_months`: Number of historical milestone snapshots observed in OCMS (pre-transition monitoring depth).
6. `pre_paimana_schedule_revision_count`: Number of official commissioning date shifts ($DOC_k \ne DOC_{k-1}$) recorded prior to April 2025.
7. `pre_paimana_cost_revision_count`: Number of sanctioned cost revisions ($|Cost_k - Cost_{k-1}| > 0.05$ Cr) recorded prior to April 2025.
8. `pre_paimana_max_cost_escalation_pct`: Maximum historical cost growth percentage accumulated prior to PAIMANA entry.
9. `pre_paimana_max_schedule_slippage_months`: Maximum historical commissioning delay (in months) accumulated prior to PAIMANA entry.

Explicitly Prohibited Variables:
- `project_id`, `legacy_ocms_code`, `project_name` (Overfitting / identity leakage).
- `prediction_month`, `report_month` (Artificial temporal clustering).
- Any PAIMANA-era target label or physical progress outcome occurring at $t > \text{2025-03}$ (Target leakage).

3. EVALUATION OF CLUSTERING ALGORITHMS
========================================================================================================================

Candidate Algorithms Evaluated on Cohort A ($N = 1,442$):

1. K-Means Clustering (Standard Euclidean on Scaled Space) — [RECOMMENDED]
   - Preprocessing: Log-transformation of monetary costs + `StandardScaler()` on continuous features.
   - Strengths: Highly interpretable centroid vectors, convex partition boundaries, fast computation, deterministic inference
     projection via Euclidean distance to nearest centroid $\arg\min_k \|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2$.
   - Suitability: EXCELLENT.

2. Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (Ward Linkage)
   - Preprocessing: `StandardScaler()`.
   - Strengths: Does not assume spherical clusters; produces dendrogram structure.
   - Weaknesses: Produces unbalanced cluster allocations (e.g. at $k=4$, one cluster had $N=13$ projects, creating unstable benchmarks).
   - Suitability: MODERATE.

3. DBSCAN / Density-Based Clustering
   - Preprocessing: RobustScaler.
   - Weaknesses: Severe density gradient between small road projects and massive railway corridors; marked 25% of projects as noise.
   - Suitability: POOR for benchmarking (cannot leave projects unclustered without benchmarks).

4. K-Prototypes / Mixed-Type Clustering
   - Combines K-Means for numerical features with K-Modes for categorical sectors/agencies.
   - Assessment: Effective, but one-hot encoding categorical sectors into continuous PCA space followed by K-Means achieves identical
     outcomes with superior standard scikit-learn portability.

+-----+-----------+------------------+-------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------+-------------------------------+

Selection Justification:
- $k=2$ produces high silhouette score (0.5356) but splits projects into only two broad categories (small vs mega), failing
  to separate chronic stagnation from administrative volatility.
- $k=4$ achieves the peak multi-archetype balance (Silhouette = 0.4111) while maintaining well-sized clusters (smallest cluster $N=71$),
  well above the minimum statistical stability threshold ($N \ge 30$).
- $k \ge 5$ immediately creates isolated micro-clusters ($N = 6$), which lead to unstable benchmark estimates.
- CONCLUSION: $k = 4$ is the mathematically and operationally optimal cluster resolution.
