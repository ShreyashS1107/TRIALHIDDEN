1. DIRECT ANSWERS TO THE 12 EVALUATION QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Did archetype-based historical benchmarks provide incremental predictive value?
A1: NO. Across the primary active operational targets (`schedule_delay_3m` and
    `cost_overrun_state_3m`), adding archetype benchmarks yielded zero or slightly
    negative incremental predictive value over the frozen 75-feature PAIMANA-only baseline.

Q2: Did they improve OOT PR-AUC?
A2: NO.
    - Schedule Delay: OOT PR-AUC dropped from 0.9722 to 0.9713 (Δ = -0.0009).
    - Cost Overrun: OOT PR-AUC was an exact tie at 0.9881 vs 0.9881 (Δ = -0.0000).
    - Schedule Revision: OOT PR-AUC showed an apparent lift (+0.0121, 0.0519 to 0.0641)
      on an extremely rare target (1.19% base rate), but this was driven entirely
      by a static legacy linkage flag rather than archetype benchmarking.

Q3: Did they improve OOT ROC-AUC?
A3: NO for primary operational targets.
    - Schedule Delay: OOT ROC-AUC dropped from 0.9723 to 0.9710 (Δ = -0.0013).
    - Cost Overrun: OOT ROC-AUC dropped from 0.9895 to 0.9894 (Δ = -0.0001).
    - Schedule Revision: OOT ROC-AUC moved from 0.8264 to 0.8455 (Δ = +0.0191).

Q4: Did they improve calibration?
A4: NO. In fact, they degraded probability calibration on Out-of-Time test data:
    - Schedule Delay: Brier Score worsened from 0.0391 to 0.0394; ECE worsened from
      0.0302 to 0.0342.
    - Schedule Revision: Brier Score degraded from 0.0176 to 0.0212 (+0.0036); ECE
      degraded from 0.0202 to 0.0236 (+0.0034) due to overconfidence on legacy projects.

Q5: Were improvements consistent across targets?
A5: NO. The archetype benchmarks exhibited zero positive transfer across schedule
    delay and cost overrun, and inconsistent calibration behavior on schedule revision.

Q6: Which benchmark features contributed useful signal?
A6: Feature importance and ablation analysis revealed:
    - Historical outcome rates (delay rate, cost revision rate): Rank #21+ in importance;
      contributed negligible predictive weight (< 0.9% Gini importance).
    - Centroid distance / deviations: Added zero discriminative power.
    - `is_legacy_linked`: Contributed minor correlation with administrative revisions,
      but degraded probability calibration.

Q7: Were the benchmark features redundant with PAIMANA?
A7: YES, HIGHLY REDUNDANT. PAIMANA's direct physical/financial progress metrics
    (`months_to_original_doc_t`, `cost_escalation_pct_t`, `physical_progress_t`,
    `schedule_slippage_months_t`) already provide direct, real-time measurements
    of project velocity that strongly dominate broad historical archetype aggregates.

Q8: Was benchmark coverage sufficient?
A8: PARTIAL. While historical aggregate rates and distance metrics achieved 100%
    snapshot coverage, dynamic deviation features (`cluster_slippage_deviation_t`)
    achieved only 32.8% coverage due to missing slippage values in certain PAIMANA tables,
    rendering them operationally unsuitable for production deployment.

Q9: Did the approach generalize to Feb–Mar 2026?
A9: NO. On the frozen Feb–Mar 2026 Out-of-Time evaluation period, Model B failed
    to outperform Model A on the primary targets, confirming that archetype priors
    do not enhance out-of-time temporal generalization.

Q10: Should benchmark features enter the production ML model?
A10: NO. Incorporating archetype benchmark features into production ML would add
     architectural complexity, increase feature store maintenance overhead, and
     introduce potential failure modes without delivering measurable predictive gains.

Q11: Should OCMS archetypes remain as a contextual benchmarking layer?
A11: YES, ABSOLUTELY. While archetype benchmarks do not improve automated ML scoring,
     the 4 project archetypes established in Phase 1 (Silhouette = 0.4174) and the
     point-in-time peer benchmarks constructed in Phase 2A provide immense descriptive,
     diagnostic, and decision-support value for human ministry officials and project managers.

Q12: What should be shown to judges?
A12: Present the system as a principled, three-pillar architecture:
     1. Pillar 1 (Predictive ML): High-precision, calibrated PAIMANA-only models
        for future 3-month risk forecasting (Delay PR=0.972, Cost PR=0.988).
     2. Pillar 2 (Execution Stress Index - ESI): Deterministic index assessing
        current operational friction and delivery bottlenecks.
     3. Pillar 3 (Project Archetype & Peer Benchmarking): Longitudinal OCMS–PAIMANA
        archetype clustering (K=4) that provides contextual benchmarking and historical
        reference points to help decision-makers interpret why a project is at risk.
