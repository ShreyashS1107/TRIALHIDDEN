1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & MENTOR HYPOTHESIS EVALUATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The mentor suggested:
"Investigate projects that started around 2025 and subsequently appear in the PAIMANA era in 2026, using them to build
longitudinal trajectories and cluster-level historical benchmarks."

Empirical Findings on Mentor's Specific 2025-Start Cohort:
1. Feasibility Verdict: INFEASIBLE AS LITERALLY SPECIFIED (Sample Size N = 0).
2. Root Cause:
   - PAIMANA monitoring began in April 2025, succeeding the legacy OCMS system.
   - Projects whose sanction/approval date is in 2025 (N = 130 total in PAIMANA) were newly approved projects.
   - Consequently, EXACTLY ZERO (0) projects started in 2025 possess pre-PAIMANA historical observation snapshots in OCMS.
   - Even widening the window to include 2024 starts yields only 4 projects with pre-PAIMANA observations (0.1% portfolio coverage).
3. Practical Reinterpretation:
   The mentor's core conceptual intent was to capture "projects active across the 2025 transition boundary with multi-year
   trajectories spanning both OCMS and PAIMANA." In reality, these projects were sanctioned between 2016 and 2023 and were
   actively monitored across the 2024–2025 system transition.

We evaluated 5 rigorous candidate cohorts to establish the statistically optimal foundation for project clustering.

4. IN-DEPTH COHORT EVALUATION
========================================================================================================================

Cohort A: Comprehensive Overlapping Trajectory Cohort (N = 1,442) — [RECOMMENDED]
- Strengths:
  * Maximizes statistical sample size (1,442 unique projects, 9,714 total ML snapshots).
  * Encompasses 100% of all verified deterministic OCMS–PAIMANA linked infrastructure.
  * Zero missingness in original sanctioned costs; 99.7% completeness in baseline dates.
  * Provides sufficient sample density across key sub-sectors (Highways N=813, Railways N=215, Energy N=260) to support
    robust, multi-archetype clustering ($k=4$ or $k=5$) without creating micro-clusters ($N < 30$).
- Limitations:
  * Approximately 34.6% of projects (501 / 1,442) have exactly 2 pre-PAIMANA snapshots, reflecting projects entering OCMS
    tables in 2022–2023.

Cohort B & B2: 2025 / 2024–2025 Starts (N = 0 / N = 4) — [REJECTED]
- Evaluation: Statistically non-viable. Filtering for projects approved in 2025 eliminates all historical depth because
  projects approved in 2025 did not exist in pre-2025 OCMS archives.

Cohort C1 & C2: Substantial (N = 643) / Deep (N = 272) History Cohorts — [REJECTED FOR PRIMARY CLUSTERING]
- Strengths: High trajectory depth (mean 5.8 to 8.3 pre-PAIMANA observations).
- Limitations: Restricting clustering to $N = 643$ or $N = 272$ discards >55% to >80% of overlapping projects, severely
  reducing portfolio coverage (down to 23.5% and 9.9%), and starving the clustering model of recent highway/road projects.

Cohort D: Balanced Continuity Cohort (N = 735) — [RESERVED AS BENCHMARK VALIDATION SUBSET]
- Strengths: High dual-regime observation density ($\ge 2$ pre-PAIMANA and $\ge 6$ PAIMANA snapshots; mean 11.9 PAIMANA months).
- Role: Highly valuable as an in-depth validation cohort to audit cluster trajectory stability across the transition boundary.

5. FINAL RECOMMENDATION & METHODOLOGICAL JUSTIFICATION
========================================================================================================================

RECOMMENDED COHORT: COHORT A (All 1,442 Comprehensively Linked Overlapping Projects)

Methodological Justification:
1. Complete Population Coverage: Cohort A captures the entirety of verified multi-year infrastructure in the transition corpus.
2. Unsupervised Stability: Unsupervised algorithms (K-Means, Hierarchical) require dense spatial distributions to establish
   reliable centroids and avoid overfitting to small sample outliers. $N = 1,442$ provides the ideal sample size.
3. Out-of-Time Alignment: In the frozen OOT test split (Feb–Mar 2026), Cohort A accounts for 1,867 candidate prediction snapshots
   (48.2% of OOT rows), providing substantial statistical power to measure whether cluster benchmark features generalize.
4. Generalization to Unlinked Inceptions: As designed in the clustering architecture, projects without pre-PAIMANA history
   (unmatched $N=1,005$) can be projected into the static feature subspace of these 1,442 archetypes at inference time.
