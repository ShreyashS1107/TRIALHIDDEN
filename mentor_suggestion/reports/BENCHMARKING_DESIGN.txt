1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & BENCHMARKING PHILOSOPHY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The core recommendation of the mentor is to replace broad, noisy agency-level and sector-level historical priors with
project-similarity benchmarks.

A cluster historical benchmark answers the fundamental operational question:
"What historically happened to completed infrastructure projects that shared this exact project archetype (similar scale,
timeline, and early-stage dynamics)?"

Instead of evaluating a project against all 2,394 historical completed projects or a broad sector containing thousands of
disparate sub-contracts, the project is evaluated against its structural peer group.

2. CANDIDATE BENCHMARK METRICS & MATHEMATICAL DEFINITIONS
========================================================================================================================

For any project $i$ assigned to Archetype $k$ at prediction month $t$:

Metric 1: Cluster Smoothed Historical Delay Rate (`cluster_hist_delay_rate_smoothed_t`)
- Definition: Empirical Bayes Beta-smoothed delay rate of completed projects belonging to Archetype $k$ prior to month $t$.
- Formula:
  $$\text{DelayRate}_{\text{smoothed}}(k, t) = \frac{n_{\text{delayed}}(k, <t) + M \cdot P_{\text{portfolio}}(<t)}{N_{\text{completed}}(k, <t) + M}$$
  where $M = 5.0$ pseudo-observations and $P_{\text{portfolio}}(<t)$ is the global historical delay rate (82.66%).

Metric 2: Cluster Historical Median Delay (`cluster_hist_median_delay_months_t`)
- Definition: Median completed schedule delay (in calendar months) among historical projects in Archetype $k$.
- Role: Provides a robust, non-parametric baseline of expected total duration expansion for this archetype.

Metric 3: Cluster Historical Mean Delay (`cluster_hist_mean_delay_months_t`)
- Definition: Arithmetic mean completed schedule delay in months.

Metric 4: Cluster Distance to Centroid (`cluster_distance_to_centroid_t`)
- Definition: Normalized Euclidean distance from the project's standardized feature vector $\mathbf{z}_i$ to the centroid
  $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k$ of its assigned archetype:
  $$d_i = \|\mathbf{z}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2$$
- Role: Quantifies archetype typicality vs outlier status. Highly atypical projects within an archetype exhibit greater
  tail risk.

Metric 5: Benchmark Schedule Slippage Deviation (`cluster_slippage_deviation_t`)
- Definition: The project's current active schedule slippage minus the historical median slippage for its archetype:
  $$\Delta_{\text{slippage}}(i, t) = \text{schedule\_slippage\_months}_i(t) - \text{cluster\_hist\_median\_delay}(k, t)$$
- Role: Orthogonal risk metric measuring whether a project is tracking better or worse than historical expectations for
  its specific archetype.

Metric 6: Cluster Historical Stagnation Frequency (`cluster_hist_stagnation_rate_t`)
- Definition: Historical frequency of physical progress stalls ($\ge 3$ consecutive months of zero progress) within the cluster.

3. REDUNDANCY AUDIT: BENCHMARK FEATURES VS. 75 PAIMANA FEATURES
========================================================================================================================

In the earlier OCMS enrichment experiment, raw historical prior features failed to provide incremental lift because:
1. High Collinearity with Project Age & Slippage:
   - `pre_paimana_observation_months` had $r = 0.773$ with `project_age_months_t` and $r = 0.515$ with `schedule_slippage_months_t`.
   - `pre_paimana_max_schedule_slippage_months` had $r = 0.480$ with `project_age_months_t`.
   - In tree ensembles (Random Forest), trees preferred splitting on the active continuous `schedule_slippage_months_t`
     variable, rendering raw historical counters redundant.
2. High Cardinality Noise: Agency priors spanned 1,065 distinct agency names, most with $N < 3$ projects.

How the Project-Similarity Benchmark Architecture Solves Redundancy:
1. Low Dimensionality & High Density: Replaces 1,065 sparse agency buckets with 4 robust, dense archetype clusters ($N \ge 71$).
2. Relative Cohort Normalization: Instead of supplying the raw historical counter, the model receives the relative deviation
   (`cluster_slippage_deviation_t`), which measures structural underperformance relative to peers.
3. Orthogonal Structural Context: A ₹4,000 Cr project experiencing a 6-month delay is well within normal parameters for
   Archetype 0 (Centroid Delay = 23.7m), whereas a 6-month delay on a ₹150 Cr rapid-execution highway (Archetype 1, Centroid Delay = 1.5m)
   represents severe abnormal distress.
