4. OFF-DIAGONAL ANALYSIS: WHY ESI PROVIDES UNIQUE VALUE
==========================================================================================

A. Group A: High Execution Stress (ESI >= 0.55) with Low/Medium Integrated Risk (< 0.55)
   Count: 698 project-months (4.43% of all observations)

   Why does this happen? (Operational Blind-Spot of Predictive Models):
   - These are typically early-stage or mid-stage infrastructure projects whose original sanctioned
     completion date is still 2 to 4 years in the future.
   - Because the deadline is far away, the calibrated Schedule Delay model (which evaluates delay at
     t+3 relative to DOC) correctly predicts low/moderate probability of terminal breach in the near term.
   - HOWEVER, on the construction site at month t, the project has suffered 4 consecutive months of
     ZERO physical progress, or has spent 45% of its budget with only 8% physical realization (acute divergence).
   - ESI captures this immediate operational crisis years before terminal delay models would trigger!

B. Group B: Low Execution Stress (ESI < 0.35) with High/Critical Integrated Risk (>= 0.55)
   Count: 3,454 project-months (21.90% of all observations)

   Why does this happen? (Historical Legacy vs Active Site Momentum):
   - These are mature legacy projects that incurred massive schedule delays (e.g. 5-10 years delayed)
     in past decades.
   - Because they are already past their original DOC, the predictive Schedule Delay model assigns them
     P(Delay) = 1.0 (Critical Risk).
   - HOWEVER, under active site monitoring at month t, the project is executing smoothly: advancing at
     2.5% to 4.0% per month, billing aligned with work, and actively marching toward its revised commissioning date.
   - ESI correctly reports low operational friction (Nominal), allowing project managers to distinguish
     rehabilitated/active legacy projects from actively stalled disasters.

6. CONCLUSION
==========================================================================================

The Operational Execution-Stress Index (ESI) is NOT redundant with the existing production models.
It provides a complementary, highly actionable real-time operational perspective that empowers MoSPI
to detect site execution bottlenecks early without distorting terminal probability models.
