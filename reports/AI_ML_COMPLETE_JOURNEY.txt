The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) and the Infrastructure and Project Monitoring Division
(IPMD) monitor all major central infrastructure projects costing ₹150 Crore or more. The objective is to build a

Government monitoring reports record point-in-time administrative status, not clean ML labels.
- There was no column stating "this project is high risk."
- Only completed projects had a final delivery date (actual_completion_date).
- Ongoing projects were actively evolving.
- Some projects disappeared from Table 6 temporarily or permanently without an explicit completion entry.

Therefore, constructing leak-free, forward-looking point-in-time targets became a primary technical undertaking.

C. Longitudinal Continuity: Removing rows would break the continuous monthly trajectory required for computing lags and velocities.

Key Findings from eda/eda_report.txt and their Machine Learning Implications:

1. High Baseline Schedule Delay Rate:
   - Finding: 62.69% of projects with valid schedule data had breached or extended their original completion date.
     Mean portfolio delay was 19.1 months (median: 10.0 months, max: 300.0 months).
   - ML Implication: Standard classification accuracy is misleading. Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC) and ROC-AUC

2. Prolonged Construction Stagnation:
   - Finding: 30.46% of projects remained stagnant (0% progress increase) for >= 3 consecutive months; 8.57% were stagnant

3. Low Median Progress Velocity:
   - Finding: Mean monthly progress velocity was +1.63%/month, but median velocity was +0.00%/month (46.37% of monthly

4. Cost Escalation Dynamics:
   - Finding: 29.99% of projects escalated above original sanctioned budget; 56.66% remained unchanged; 13.17% decreased.
   - ML Implication: Cost overrun state is a structural, persistent condition; acute new cost escalation events are rare shocks.

5. Progress vs Expenditure Divergence:
   - Finding: In 7.92% of observations (1,707 snapshots), cumulative expenditure percentage exceeded physical progress by > 20%.
     271 observations exhibited high capital spend (>50% cost) with under 10% physical progress.
   - ML Implication: Capital absorption outpacing physical execution is a strong operational distress flag.

6. Reporting Cohort Shift (July–November 2025):
   - Finding: Active projects monitored in Table 6 dipped to ~800 projects in Jul–Nov 2025 (focused cohort) before expanding
     back to ~1,900 in Dec 2025 – Jun 2026.
   - ML Implication: Target construction and temporal splitting must account for unobserved project exits during this window.

PART 6 — THE TARGET FEASIBILITY INVESTIGATION: WHAT SHOULD WE PREDICT?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why not predict final project completion directly (actual completion date > original completion date)?
1. Sample Size Limitation: Only 350 completed projects exist in Table 3. Training an ML model on 350 historical completions
   cannot generalize to 2,741 ongoing projects.
2. Unknowable Ground Truth: Ongoing projects have not yet reached final delivery; their ultimate completion status is unknown.
3. Administrative Date Slippage: Revised completion dates change dynamically over time and often lag ground reality.
4. Contemporaneous Leakage: Using revised completion date at month t to predict delay at month t is circular and invalid.

The Solution: Fixed-Horizon Point-in-Time Prediction:
Formulate the problem as: "Given information available at prediction month t, what is the probability that the project
will experience an operational delay at horizon t + 3 months?"
This yielded 15,769 candidate prediction slices across 12 monthly epochs (April 2025 to March 2026).

Four Frozen Target Definitions (target_labels_v2/target_label_report_v2.txt):

1. Primary Operational Pillar: schedule_delay_3m
   - Meaning: Does the project breach its sanctioned original completion date within the 3-month future window (t+3)?
   - Positive Class (1): Ongoing in Table 6 at t+3 with t+3 > original_DOC, OR completed in Table 3 with actual_DOC > original_DOC.
   - Negative Class (0): Ongoing in Table 6 at t+3 with t+3 <= original_DOC, OR completed in Table 3 with actual_DOC <= original_DOC.
   - Distribution: Labelled = 11,893 (75.4%) | Positives = 6,489 (54.56%) | Negatives = 5,404 (45.44%) | Unlabelled = 3,876 (24.6%).

2. Secondary Administrative Pillar: schedule_revision_3m
   - Meaning: Is a new delayed completion date (> original DOC) newly sanctioned in window (t, t+3]?
   - Positive Class (1): New revised DOC > original DOC observed in (t, t+3] that was not known at month t.
   - Distribution: Labelled = 11,893 (75.4%) | Positives = 279 (2.35%) | Negatives = 11,614 (97.65%).

3. Secondary Financial Pillar: cost_overrun_state_3m
   - Meaning: At t+3, is reported cost higher than original sanctioned budget?
   - Positive Class (1): revised_cost(t+3) > original_cost + 0.01 Cr.
   - Distribution: Labelled = 12,154 (77.1%) | Positives = 3,768 (31.00%) | Negatives = 8,386 (69.00%).

4. Secondary Financial Event: cost_revision_event_3m
   - Meaning: Does a NEW upward cost escalation occur in (t, t+3] exceeding max(original_cost, revised_cost_t)?
   - Positive Class (1): revised_cost(t+3) > max(original_cost, revised_cost_t) + 0.01 Cr.
   - Distribution: Labelled = 12,154 (77.1%) | Positives = 543 (4.47%) | Negatives = 11,611 (95.53%).

The Unresolved Exit Investigation:

  focused cohort reporting window, and 1,901 of these projects reappeared in subsequent months.
- Anti-Leakage Rule: These records were preserved as NaN and excluded from training loss to prevent creating fake labels.

Why Chronological Splitting Was Mandatory:
Random k-fold splitting mixes past and future observations of the same project, causing extreme lookahead leakage.
Chronological splitting simulates true production forecasting: models trained on past data predict future unseen months.
Longitudinal project overlap across splits (1,404 projects present across all 3 splits) is valid and intended because
features at month t only utilize historical data <= t.

PART 24 — DEFENSE Q&A: IF A JUDGE ASKS WHY?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Why didn't you randomly split the data into 80/20 train/test sets?
A1: Infrastructure project monitoring is longitudinal. Shuffling project snapshots randomly causes massive data leakage
    by placing future observations in training and past observations in testing. Chronological splitting (Train: Apr–Nov 2025,
    Val: Dec 2025–Jan 2026, OOT: Feb–Mar 2026) strictly prevents lookahead bias and simulates true production forecasting.

Q2: Why a 3-month prediction horizon instead of predicting final completion directly?
A2: Only 350 completed projects exist in Table 3. Training an ML model on 350 completed projects would fail on 2,741 ongoing
    projects. The 3-month point-in-time formulation generated 15,769 valid prediction slices across 12 monthly epochs.

Q3: How did you prove that the Random Forest model's 0.9709 OOT ROC-AUC was not data leakage?
A3: We conducted an ablation audit: removing ALL 8 schedule-derived features left a model that achieved 0.8921 OOT ROC-AUC
    purely from physical progress velocity, stagnation streaks, and capital burn rates.

Q4: Why was Random Forest selected over Gradient Boosted Trees?
A4: Random Forest achieved superior probability calibration on validation (Brier 0.0488 vs 0.1724) and demonstrated
    smoother temporal stability.

Q5: Why did you calibrate probabilities using Platt Sigmoid rather than Isotonic Regression?
A5: Isotonic regression produced step-wise plateaus on validation. Platt Sigmoid provides a smooth, continuous monotonic
    mapping that reduced OOT Brier score by 42.2% (0.0678 -> 0.0389) and generalized better to unseen holdouts.

Q6: Why did the cost revision event model fail on OOT data?
A6: Our diagnostic audit uncovered a 10x base-rate collapse (21.9% in April 2025 -> 0.9% in February 2026) and a structural
    profile inversion: training revisions occurred on mature escalated projects, while OOT revisions occurred on new projects.

Q7: Why didn't you hide or discard the failed cost revision model silently?
A7: Responsible machine learning requires transparent failure diagnosis. We conducted a root-cause audit, documented
    the failure, and moved the target to RESEARCH_ONLY, preserving the integrity of the active scoring engine.

Q8: Why is the integrated risk score not called a probability?
A8: The integrated score combines three distinct operational dimensions (schedule delay probability, financial overrun
    state, and administrative rescheduling warning). It is an operational prioritization index in [0, 1], not a single
    univariate probability of overall failure.

Q9: How will the backend engineering team consume your ML results?
A9: The backend does not run ML models. It ingests the standardized integrated_risk_scores.csv matrix containing project IDs,
    prediction months, component probabilities, integrated risk scores, risk bands, and dominant risk drivers.
