--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why didn't you simply combine OCMS and PAIMANA into one big dataset?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1: OCMS (2011–2025) and PAIMANA (2025–2026) represent two different reporting eras
    with different reporting cadences, variable definitions, and schema structures.
    Blindly merging them causes severe temporal distribution shifts and data corruption.
    Instead, we established a clean longitudinal linkage over 1,442 validated projects
    to extract point-in-time historical profiles without compromising data integrity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q2: Why is PAIMANA the authoritative source for the production ML model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A2: PAIMANA is the active, modern flash-reporting platform actively utilized by MoSPI.
    Its monthly reports contain standardized physical progress velocities, expenditure
    ratios, and milestone tracking that directly represent current project momentum.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q3: Why did you cluster projects?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A3: Megaprojects differ fundamentally: a ₹50,000 Cr multi-decade high-speed rail corridor
    behaves completely differently from a ₹300 Cr 2-lane highway bypass. Clustering
    allowed us to group projects into distinct physical archetypes based on scale, duration,
    and historical volatility to enable meaningful peer-group comparisons.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q4: What does a "project archetype" mean?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A4: An archetype is an empirical cluster representing a distinct category of project
    execution behavior. Our unsupervised clustering discovered 4 stable archetypes:
    - Cluster 0: High Cost-Revision Volatility (₹495 Cr, 2.64 pre-PAIMANA revisions)
    - Cluster 1: Standard Rapid Linear Infrastructure (₹795 Cr, 41m duration)
    - Cluster 2: Severe Legacy Stagnation (₹1,566 Cr, 251 months median delay)
    - Cluster 3: Mega Capital Infrastructure (₹7,588 Cr, 96m duration)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q5: Did clustering improve predictive ML accuracy?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A5: Under the controlled out-of-time experiment, archetype benchmark features did not
    provide clear incremental predictive value beyond the 75 PAIMANA features. Schedule
    delay PR-AUC went from 0.9722 to 0.9713, and cost overrun stayed tied at 0.9881.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q6: If clustering did not improve ML accuracy, why keep it?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A6: Because AI systems serve humans, not just algorithms. While machine learning models
    do not need broad archetype averages (since they already use fine-grained project
    velocities), human ministry officials and project managers greatly benefit from
    archetype benchmarking to understand how a project compares against its historical peers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q7: What is the Execution Stress Index (ESI)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A7: ESI is a deterministic, transparent engineering index that quantifies real-time
    execution friction (e.g., physical-financial progress divergence, milestone stalling,
    and quarterly velocity deceleration) occurring in the current reporting month.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q8: Is ESI fed into the ML model as a feature?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A8: No. ESI is kept strictly independent of the predictive ML models. This prevents
    circular feature feedback and ensures ESI serves as an objective, rule-based audit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q9: How do you prevent temporal data leakage?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A9: We enforce strict point-in-time feature engineering (`evidence_date < prediction_month`),
    fit all preprocessing transformers strictly on the training partition, isolate
    target labels in a separate dataset, and run automated leakage verification audits
    (0 violations across 15,769 snapshot rows).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q10: Why use chronological Out-of-Time (OOT) testing instead of random train/test splits?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A10: Random train/test splits cause severe leakage in longitudinal data because future
     snapshots leak into training folds. Chronological OOT testing (Train: Apr–Nov 2025,
     Val: Dec 2025–Jan 2026, OOT: Feb–Mar 2026) faithfully simulates how the system
     performs when deployed in the real world to predict future months.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q11: What are your three active ML prediction targets?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A11: 1. `schedule_delay_3m`: Will the project experience a milestone delay in 3 months?
     2. `cost_overrun_state_3m`: Will the project be in a cost overrun state in 3 months?
     3. `schedule_revision_3m`: Will authorities formally revise the completion date in 3 months?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q12: Why was `cost_revision_event_3m` removed from the operational risk engine?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A12: Rigorous empirical evaluation showed that cost revision events occur irregularly
     and lack short-term (3-month) predictive signals. Adhering to scientific integrity,
     we classified it as RESEARCH_ONLY and removed it from operational scoring rather
     than presenting a weak model.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q13: What are the 75 features in the production ML model?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A13: The production feature universe contains 71 numerical features (physical progress,
     lagged velocities, expenditure ratios, cumulative spending, slippage in months,
     project age, and agency aggregations) plus 4 categorical features (size category,
     current schedule status, structure version, and table source).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q14: What happens when a new monthly government flash report arrives?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A14: The automated pipeline ingests the report, computes the 75 point-in-time features,
     evaluates real-time ESI stress, generates calibrated 3-month forward risk probabilities
     via the frozen ML models, and maps the project to its historical archetype for peer
     benchmarking in the executive dashboard.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q15: What is the exact difference between Predictive Risk and Execution Stress?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A15: - Predictive Risk (ML): Forward-looking (Probabilistic forecast of what will happen
       3 months ahead).
     - Execution Stress (ESI): Present-looking (Deterministic assessment of friction
       and physical-financial divergence happening right now).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q16: What does OCMS contribute to the final deployed system?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A16: OCMS powers the Historical Intelligence Layer: providing 4 validated project
     archetypes, historical duration baselines, peer delay distributions, and longitudinal
     continuity indicators that give context to executive decision-makers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q17: What would you do with more data in future iterations?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A17: With multi-year prospective PAIMANA data, we would expand to 6-month and 12-month
     long-range forecasting horizons, incorporate text-based contractor inspection logs
     via NLP, and build agency-specific risk calibration curves.
